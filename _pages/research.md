My research develops autonomous and AI-assisted systems for endovascular stroke intervention. It combines medical robotics, reinforcement learning, computer vision, and patient-specific simulation to investigate safe robotic navigation through complex cerebrovascular anatomy.

## AI-Assisted Endovascular Robotics

I investigate intelligent robotic assistance for mechanical thrombectomy, with the aim of making specialist stroke treatment more accessible and scalable. This work includes autonomous guidewire and catheter navigation, task decomposition, and safety-aware control.

## Reinforcement Learning

My work uses reinforcement learning to train agents in patient-specific vascular simulation environments. I am particularly interested in hierarchical and multi-agent approaches that can separate complex procedures into interpretable, reusable skills.

## Perception and Navigation

Reliable perception is central to autonomy in image-guided intervention. I develop methods for catheter tip tracking and navigation from fluoroscopic images, including transformer-based approaches designed for real-time use.

## Evaluation and Translation

I also work on standardised models and effectiveness metrics for evaluating robotic thrombectomy navigation. The goal is to support reproducible research and responsible translation from simulation to clinically useful systems.
